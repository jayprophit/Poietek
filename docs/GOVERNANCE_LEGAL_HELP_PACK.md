# Governance, Legal and Help Drafting Pack

Status: product specification and legal drafting brief. **It is not legal advice,
not approved terms, and not an effective public policy.** Before launch, counsel
and accountable company officers must provide the legal entity name, address,
contact routes, target jurisdictions, age floor, governing law, provider list,
commission, payment/payout rules, retention schedule and approval evidence.

## 1. Terms of Service drafting requirements

The final terms must cover eligibility, accounts, devices, acceptable use,
availability, offline and optional online services, paid tiers, third-party
providers, suspension, termination, warranties, liability, indemnities, dispute
routes, governing law and change notice. Product controls must record the exact
policy version, decision, time and actor. Silence or application use is not stored
as acceptance.

### User content and ownership

The intended clause is that users retain ownership of content they own. Poietek
does not acquire ownership merely because a work is created, stored, processed,
shared, streamed or sold using the service. Poietek retains its software, brand,
service design, documentation and other company intellectual property.

The service may need a non-exclusive, purpose-limited licence to store/process,
display to the user's selected audience, stream a selected release, deliver a
purchased licence and moderate for safety. The final text must define duration,
territory, sublicensing to essential processors, withdrawal/deletion consequences
and backups. It must not promise that every uploader owns every underlying right.

Users remain responsible for permissions in samples, compositions, masters,
performances, appearances, artwork, footage, trademarks and confidential data.
Employment, commissioned work, joint creation and prior contracts can change who
owns or controls a work. Rights disputes require an evidence and appeal process.

## 2. Community rules and acceptable use

The controlled rules must address harassment, threats, hate, exploitation,
non-consensual intimate content, child sexual abuse material, violent extremist
content, scams, impersonation, spam, malware, privacy violations, illegal goods,
copyright infringement and attempts to defeat safety/security. Define report,
triage, emergency escalation, content restriction, account action, reasons,
appeal, restoration and repeat-abuse handling.

Hosted, federated and peer-to-peer modes need separate enforcement boundaries.
Federation agreements must define server blocking and shared reports; P2P design
must support user control, revocation and unlawful-content response.

## 3. Content, copyright and provenance

Provide rights declarations at upload/publication, accessible notice and
counter-notice routes, repeat-infringer handling where applicable, preservation
of evidence, dispute status and restoration. A hash, signature, timestamp or
blockchain anchor is provenance evidence only; it does not determine ownership.
Do not present registration submissions or split proposals as accepted without
an authoritative external reference and observation time.

## 4. Live streaming and Poietek TV

Channel owners must declare the necessary music, performance, film, contributor,
location and broadcast permissions. The operational pack needs stream safety,
delay/interruption controls, viewer reports, moderator tools, appeals, emergency
escalation, record retention, captioning and accessible playback. Donations and
live shopping require separate payment, consumer and safeguarding terms.

## 5. Marketplace, fees and payouts

Buyer and seller terms must distinguish ownership from a licence, show total
price and taxes, describe compatibility and usage rights, preserve contractual
information, obtain any required immediate-digital-delivery consent, and explain
cancellation/refund/remedy routes. Sellers require identity/trader checks,
payment/tax information, rights warranties, listing rules and payout controls.

No commission is approved in this build. The final fee policy must state the
rate/calculation, taxes, payment fees, currency conversion, reserves, refunds,
chargebacks, payout timing and change notice. Platform reporting duties and a
seller copy of reported information must be implemented where applicable.

## 6. Privacy, security and children

Use privacy by design: purpose limitation, data minimisation, high privacy
defaults, encryption boundaries, access control, retention/deletion, export,
correction and consent withdrawal. Publish processor/provider purposes,
international transfers, cookies/analytics choices, incident routes and contact
details. Remote AI and provider model training default to disabled or per-request
consent.

Because social, video, messaging, live and marketplace services may be accessed
by children, public launch needs an age-appropriate design assessment, best-
interests analysis, age assurance proportionate to risk, high privacy defaults
and safeguarding operations. Do not infer that a general “13+” statement alone
satisfies these duties.

## 7. AI use

Users choose whether to use AI and which configured provider/model receives
project context. State what leaves the device, purpose, retention and provider
terms before each remote request. Generated or materially manipulated media must
carry appropriate disclosure/provenance when required. AI changes need preview,
explicit acceptance and undo. Training on user projects is denied by default
unless a separate explicit consent and lawful basis are approved.

## 8. Accessibility

Target WCAG 2.2 AA across desktop, touch and web surfaces. Include keyboard and
assistive-technology operation, focus/contrast/scaling/reduced motion, status not
communicated by colour alone, captions/transcripts for recorded media and an
operational plan for live captions. Publish an accessibility statement, known
limitations and contact/response route.

## 9. Governance and transparency

Define the accountable company roles, policy-change process, creator/community
participation, moderator authority, conflicts, appeals and public transparency
reports. Reports should explain moderation volumes/outcomes, automated tools,
authority requests, appeals, restoration and safety-risk work without exposing
personal or security-sensitive information.

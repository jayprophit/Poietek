# Volume 08 — Rights, Licensing & Publishing

Document ID: `POI-VOL-08`
Edition: `1.0.0`
Primary domains: `CAP-08`, `CAP-09`, `CAP-12`, `DOM-RIGHTS`, `DOM-PUBLISHING`

## Scope and legal boundary

This volume defines contributor passports, splits, agreements, clearances,
licences, provenance evidence, registrations, release preparation, distribution
status, marketplace orders, royalty statements and corrections. Poietek records
facts and workflows; it is not a court, collecting society, publisher, payment
network or automatic copyright authority.

## Contributor passport

Each contributor record can hold verified/unverified identity references,
preferred credit, roles, territory/contact policy, organisation affiliations,
contribution evidence, agreement links and privacy controls. Public identity is
separate from private legal/payment data.

## Splits and acceptance

A split proposal names the work/version, participants, roles, percentages/points,
scope, timestamp and proposer. States include draft, proposed, partially accepted,
disputed, superseded and fully accepted. Full acceptance requires explicit
participant/authorized evidence and immutable references; entering percentages
that total 100 does not create legal acceptance.

## Rights and clearance records

- Composition, master, performance, artwork, video, sample and software/plugin
  rights remain distinguishable.
- Sample/third-party assets record source, licence, scope, territory, term,
  restrictions, attribution and evidence.
- Unclear, expired, disputed or missing evidence blocks destinations that require
  clearance.
- Optional blockchain anchors record hashes/receipts only; they do not determine
  legal ownership or replace an agreement.

## Registration and publishing workflows

1. Select a versioned work/release candidate.
2. Validate contributors, titles, identifiers, splits, clearances and metadata.
3. Choose a destination profile and generate required derivative media.
4. Run technical, rights and creative-intent preflight separately.
5. Obtain authorized approval and submit through a configured adapter.
6. Record idempotency key, external reference, receipt and authoritative status.
7. Poll or receive signed webhooks for acceptance, rejection or correction.
8. Preserve the submitted package and every subsequent amendment.

Submission is not acceptance. A release-ready score cannot bypass missing
authority, external receipts or required unmeasured standards.

## Licensing and commerce

Listings describe the exact asset/version, seller authority, licence grant,
restrictions, territory, term, price/tax/currency, refund/support and fulfilment
method. Order states distinguish intent, authorization, settlement, failure,
refund and chargeback. Fulfilment requires authoritative settlement evidence.
Purchase/licence evidence never becomes a blanket claim of ownership.

## Royalties and statements

Ledgers record source reports, periods, currencies, deductions, allocations,
exchange policy, payable status and correction lineage. Imported statements keep
their source and confidence. Payment instruction, payable balance and settled
payment are separate states with audit evidence.

## Privacy, security and audit

Sensitive identity, tax, banking, contracts and minors' data use purpose limits,
field-level access, encryption, retention/deletion rules and auditable disclosure.
Rights/publishing/commerce actions require stronger authorization, idempotency
and evidence retention than ordinary project editing.

## Current status and acceptance

Serializable contributor, split, rights, registration, evidence, provenance,
commerce and release-readiness contracts with strict validators are foundations.
No external registration acceptance, payment settlement, blockchain transaction,
royalty collection or distribution service is claimed. Production requires legal
review, configured authorities, receipts/webhooks, idempotency, corrections,
tenant isolation, regional/consumer/tax policy and disaster-recovery evidence.

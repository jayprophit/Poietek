### About the 5-star benchmark

I implemented it, but with one non-negotiable rule: **Poietek doesn't get awarded 5★ because we want it to be 5★.**

Right now, the audited prototype is **not** 5★ against Ableton, Logic, Bitwig, Melodyne, Serato, rekordbox or DaVinci Resolve. Pretending otherwise would make the benchmark useless.

Instead, I created a certification system where:

- **1★** = prototype/concept.
- **2★** = basic end-to-end functionality.
- **3★** = genuinely production usable.
- **4★** = competitive with established category leaders.
- **5★** = objectively meets or exceeds the strongest relevant competitor, passes reliability/performance tests, and survives structured user testing.

That matters because different competitors are the benchmark for different pieces. Ableton provides mature Arrangement/Session workflows and extensibility; Bitwig provides an especially useful benchmark for deep modulation and separate-process plugin crash protection; Logic provides an important Mac/iPad and touch-production benchmark. 

Melodyne is the pitch/audio-note benchmark because its current published capabilities include note-based pitch/timing/formant editing, multitrack editing and polyphonic Direct Note Access.  Serato and rekordbox provide the DJ benchmark around four-deck/stems/DVS/hardware and set-preparation/device workflows.  DaVinci Resolve is the obvious integrated video/VFX/color/audio-post benchmark, with dedicated Edit, Fusion, Color and Fairlight environments. 

The goal is therefore **5★ across every declared Poietek flagship category**, but every star has to be backed by evidence.

### Contributor approvals can now be cryptographically signed

I added an EIP-712-based rights-approval design. EIP-712 is specifically designed for signing structured, meaningful data rather than arbitrary opaque bytes. 

So instead of somebody signing something meaningless like:

```text
0xf84b9218c....
```

they can approve a structured record equivalent to:

```text
Poietek Rights Approval

Project:
Midnight Drive

Rights Manifest:
Version 4

Contributor:
Party_83920

Role:
Producer / Performer

Manifest Hash:
SHA256...

Expires:
...

APPROVE
```

The signature binds them to **that exact version and hash**.

If the agreement changes, version 5 is a different approval.

Smart-contract wallets are supported too through ERC-1271-compatible signature verification. 

### Payment and royalty tracking gets a clever privacy layer

Rather than putting everyone's earnings publicly on-chain, Poietek can create a **Merkle settlement root**.

Imagine a settlement contains:

```text
Artist A       £438.17
Producer B     £121.42
Writer C       £76.09
Company D      £52.20
```

Those lines remain private.

Poietek cryptographically hashes the complete settlement into a root:

```text
Settlement Batch #9382

Merkle root:
0x837ACF...

Rights Manifest:
v4

Period:
July 2027

Blockchain:
Confirmed
```

Each contributor receives their own line and an inclusion proof.

That means they can verify:

> "My £121.42 allocation really was included in the settlement Poietek anchored."

without seeing everybody else's finances.

OpenZeppelin's current contract libraries include Merkle-proof utilities, EIP-712 handling, signature checking, access control, nonces and other primitives suitable for implementing this kind of system rather than inventing custom cryptography.
